> [!def] [[Definition 7 (Sigma-Algebra)]]: Let $X$ be a set and $\mathcal A$ a family of subsets of $X$. It is a $\sigma$-algebra (or $\sigma$-field) if:
> i. $\emptyset\in\mathcal A$.
> ii. If $A\in\mathcal A$, then $A^c=X-A\in\mathcal A$.
> iii. If $(A_n)$ is a sequence in $\mathcal A$, then $\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\in\mathcal A$.

^nt-47763eaa977eba11

###### Examples.

i. For any set $X$, $\{\emptyset,X\}$ and $\mathcal P(X)$ are $\sigma$-algebras.
ii. On $\mathbb N$, $\{\emptyset,\{0,2,4,\ldots\},\{1,3,5,\ldots\},\mathbb N\}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra.
iii. On uncountable $X$, $\mathcal A=\{Y\subseteq X:Y\text{ is countable or }Y^c\text{ is countable}\}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra. Recall that a countable union of countable sets is countable.
