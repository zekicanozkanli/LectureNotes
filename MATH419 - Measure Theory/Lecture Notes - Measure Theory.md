
 Integralleme etrafında measure theory 'yi kurmaya çalışalım. Riemann integralini anmakla başlayalım. Riemann integral is essentially defined to be the limit of finite sums of products of twos, one of which is the length of the each subintervals of a closed interval of subset of $\mathbb R$, and the other one is the integrable function $f$ evaluated at an arbitrary point $\xi_i$ from the corresponding subinterval. We write $$\mathcal I_{\mathcal R} := \sum_i f(\xi_i) \Delta x_i.$$
To $\mathcal I_{\mathcal R}$ exists, we require $f$ to not vary too much in a corresponding subinterval, as the value $\mathcal I_{\mathcal R}$ shall not change the choice of $\xi_i$, that is, we require that $$ \forall \varepsilon >0 \ \ \exists P : \sum_i (\sup_{x \in P_i} f - \inf_{x \in P_i} f)\Delta x_i < \varepsilon.$$This requirement can be passed if it is known that $f$ is continuous on given interval, however, to define the continuity we first need to define metric spaces.

Lebesgue starts with partitioning the range of a function and collecting the pre-image of corresponding partition. For a bounded non-negative function $f: X \rightarrow \mathbb R$, we consider the preimage $$E_k = \{x : k\delta \leq f(x) \leq (k+1)\delta\}.$$Hence, on $E_k$, we now *already* know that the values of the function close to each other, regardless of the distribution of the elements of $E_k.$ ![[gemini-svg (4).svg|418]]We shall now assign a *size* (a measure $\mu$) for this set in the sense that when $X=\mathbb R$, the *size* of the set must coincides with our understanding of *length* of the interval, and that when $X = \mathbb R^2,$ it must coincides with the *area* of region, and so on... We write $$ \mathcal I_{\mathcal L} := \sum_k k\delta \ \mu(E_k).$$
