> [!thm] [[Theorem 3 (Banach-Tarski)]]: Let $B=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:x^2+y^2+z^2\le1\}$. The unit ball can be decomposed into finitely many pieces which, after translations and rotations, can be reassembled into two disjoint copies of $B$.
> More precisely, there are disjoint subsets $A_1,\ldots,A_n,B_1,\ldots,B_m\subseteq B$ and translations or rotations $T_1,\ldots,T_n,S_1,\ldots,S_m$ such that
> $$B=A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_n\cup B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_m,$$
> $$B=T_1(A_1)\cup\cdots\cup T_n(A_n),\qquad B=S_1(B_1)\cup\cdots\cup S_m(B_m).$$

###### Motivation.

The source attributes the theorem to Banach and Tarski (1924), notes its reliance on the axiom of choice, and states a general version: in $\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge3$, any two bounded subsets with nonempty interiors can be transformed into one another similarly. It presents this as an obstruction to a finitely additive invariant volume assigning positive measure to $B$. The difficulty is measuring the pieces; the source proposes restricting the domain to measurable sets.

The source supplies a [video outlining a proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s86-Z-CbaHA&t=6s).
