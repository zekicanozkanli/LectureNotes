> [!lem] [[Lemma 5.2 (Indefinite Integral as a Charge)]]: If $f$ belongs to $L$ and $\lambda$ is defined on $\mathcal X$ to $\mathbb R$ by
> $$
> \lambda(E)=\int_E f\,d\mu,
> $$
> then $\lambda$ is a charge.

> [!remark]
> The function $\lambda$ defined in (5.2) is frequently called the indefinite integral of $f$ (with respect to $\mu$). Since $\lambda$ is a charge, if $(E_n)$ is a disjoint sequence in $\mathcal X$ with union $E$, then
> $$
> \int_E f\,d\mu=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_n}f\,d\mu.
> $$
> We refer to this relation by saying that the indefinite integral of a function in $L$ is countably additive.
