> [!def] [[Definition 14 (Cells And Length In The Real Line)]]: Let $a,b\in\mathbb R$ with $a\le b$.
> i. Each of $(a,b)$, $[a,b]$, $(a,b]$, and $[a,b)$ is a cell in $\mathbb R$ with endpoints $a,b$.
> ii. If $A$ is such a cell, its length is $\ell(A)=b-a$.

^nt-d6b597ebd5580f26

###### Examples.

i. If $a=b$, $(a,b)=(a,b]=[a,b)=\emptyset$ and $[a,b]=\{a\}$.
ii. Open cells have the form $(a,b)$; closed cells have the form $[a,b]$. The other two types are half-open or half-closed.

###### Remarks.

For cells of type $(a,b]$, intersections, unions, and differences are cells of this type or disjoint unions of such cells. Complements are countable unions of such cells. The source displays
$$\mathbb R-(a,b]=(-\infty,a]\cup(b,\infty)=\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty\left(a-n,a-\frac1n\right]\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty\left(b-\frac1n,b+n\right].$$
This displayed covering is retained as printed. The source states the same closure properties for type $[a,b)$.
Cells have finite endpoints. Infinite intervals are $(-\infty,a)$, $(-\infty,a]$, $[b,\infty)$, $(b,\infty)$, and $(-\infty,\infty)$; their length is $\infty$.
