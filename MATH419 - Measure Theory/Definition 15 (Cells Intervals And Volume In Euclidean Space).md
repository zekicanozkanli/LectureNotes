> [!def] [[Definition 15 (Cells Intervals And Volume In Euclidean Space)]]: i. For $n>1$, a cell in $\mathbb R^n$ is $I=I_1\times\cdots\times I_n$, where each $I_j$ is a cell in $\mathbb R$.
> ii. Its $n$-dimensional volume is $\ell(I)=\prod_{j=1}^n\ell(I_j)$. If $I_j$ has endpoints $a_j\le b_j$, then $\ell(I)=\prod_{j=1}^n(b_j-a_j)$.

###### Examples.

i. If $I=(a,b]$, then $I^\circ=(a,b)$ and $\overline I=[a,b]$.
ii. If $I=[a,b)\times(c,d)$, then $I^\circ=(a,b)\times(c,d)$ and $\overline I=[a,b]\times[c,d]$.

###### Remarks.

Products of cells of type $(a,b]$ have intersections, unions, and differences which are cells of this type or disjoint unions of them; complements are countable unions of such cells. The source states the same for products of $[a,b)$.
An interval in $\mathbb R^n$ is $I_1\times\cdots\times I_n$ with each $I_j$ an interval in $\mathbb R$, and volume is the same product. The volume is $0$ if any factor is $0$; it is $\infty$ if all factors are nonzero and at least one is infinite. Thus $\ell(I)\in[0,\infty]$.
For any cell, the source states $\ell(I)=\ell(I^\circ)=\ell(\overline I)$.
