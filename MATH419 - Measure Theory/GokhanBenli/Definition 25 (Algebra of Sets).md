
> [!def] [[Definition 25 (Algebra of Sets)]]: A family $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal P(X)$ is an **algebra** if
> i. $\varnothing\in\mathcal A$.
> ii. $A\in\mathcal A$ implies $A^c=X\setminus A\in\mathcal A$.
> iii. For $A_1,\ldots,A_n\in\mathcal A$, $\bigcup_{i=1}^nA_i\in\mathcal A$.

###### Motivation.
The systematic construction extends measures from *simple sets* on an algebra to measures on a $\sigma$-algebra. It provides an alternative, equivalent construction of Lebesgue measure and will be used for product measures. The lecture omits some proofs because they parallel the Euclidean arguments.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: The left-open interval family has the elementary-family properties; its finite-union algebra is not a $\sigma$-algebra. (see [[Exercise 18 (The Left-Open Interval Algebra)]]).
