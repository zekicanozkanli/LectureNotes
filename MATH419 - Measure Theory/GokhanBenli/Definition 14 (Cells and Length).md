
> [!def] [[Definition 14 (Cells and Length)]]: For real $a\le b$, a **cell** in $\mathbb R$ is one of $(a,b)$, $[a,b]$, $(a,b]$, $[a,b)$, with **length** $\ell(A)=b-a$. The first type is **open**, the second is **closed**, and the remaining types are **half-open**, or **half-closed**. Cells have finite endpoints. The **infinite intervals** $(-\infty,a)$, $(-\infty,a]$, $[b,\infty)$, $(b,\infty)$, and $(-\infty,\infty)$ have length $\infty$.

> [!rem] For cells of type $(a,b]$, intersections, unions, and differences are of that type or disjoint unions of such cells; complements are countable unions of such cells. The same holds for type $[a,b)$. The source prints 
> $$
> \mathbb R\setminus(a,b]=(-\infty,a]\cup(b,\infty)=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(a-n,a-1/n]\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(b-1/n,b+n].
> $$
>  {unresolved: the last expression omits $a$ and includes points below or equal to $b$; it does not equal the displayed complement.}

###### Motivation.
Cells provide the *building blocks* for extending length, area, and volume to more general subsets.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: If $a=b$, then $(a,b)=(a,b]=[a,b)=\varnothing$, while $[a,b]=\{a\}$.
