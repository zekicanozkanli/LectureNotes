
> [!ex] [[Exercise 8 (Closed Sets Generate Borel Sets)]]: Show that $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$ is generated by the closed subsets of $\mathbb R^n$.

###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* Let $\mathcal C$ be the collection of all closed subsets of $\mathbb R^n$. We show that $\sigma(\mathcal C)=\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$.
>
> ($\subseteq$) Let $C\in\mathcal C$. Since $C$ is closed, $C^c$ is open and hence belongs to $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$. Closure under complements gives $C\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$. Thus $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$. Since $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal C$, minimality gives $\sigma(\mathcal C)\subseteq\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$.
>
> ($\supseteq$) Let $U\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open. Then $U^c$ is closed, so $U^c\in\mathcal C\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal C)$. Closure under complements gives $U=(U^c)^c\in\sigma(\mathcal C)$. Thus $\sigma(\mathcal C)$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing every open set. By the minimality of the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal C)$. The two inclusions yield $\sigma(\mathcal C)=\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$. $\blacksquare$
