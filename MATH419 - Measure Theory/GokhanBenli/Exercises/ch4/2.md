###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* For each $n\geq1$, let $E_n=(0,1-1/n]$. Each $E_n\in\mathcal A_L$, with $E_1=\varnothing$, but
>
> $$
> \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_n=(0,1).
> $$
>
> Every nonempty bounded member of $\mathcal A_L$ is a finite disjoint union of nonempty bounded intervals $(a_i,b_i]$, and hence has a largest element, namely $\max_i b_i$. Since $(0,1)$ has no largest element, it does not belong to $\mathcal A_L$. Thus $\mathcal A_L$ is not closed under countable unions and is not a $\sigma$-algebra.$\blacksquare$
