> [!def] [[Definition 4.2 (Integral of a Nonnegative Simple Function)]]: If $\varphi$ is a simple function in $M^+(X,\mathcal X)$ with the standard representation (4.1), we define the integral of $\varphi$ with respect to $\mu$ to be the extended real number
> $$
> \int\varphi\,d\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_j\mu(E_j). \tag{4.2}
> $$

> [!remark]
> We employ the convention that $0(+\infty)=0$, so the integral of the function identically $0$ is equal to $0$ whether the space has finite or infinite measure. The value of the integral of a simple function in $M^+$ is well-defined (although it may be $+\infty$), since all the $a_j$ are nonnegative, and hence we do not encounter meaningless expressions such as $(+\infty)-(+\infty)$.$^{1}$
