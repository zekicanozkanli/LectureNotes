> [!def] [[Definition 4.1 (Simple Function)]]: A real-valued function is simple if it has only a finite number of values.

###### Motivation.

“Real-valued” requires values in $\mathbb R$, so neither $+\infty$ nor $-\infty$ is allowed. “A finite number of values” describes the range, not the number of points in the domain. Bartle finds it convenient to require simple functions to have values in $\mathbb R$ rather than $\overline{\mathbb R}$.$^{1}$

A simple measurable function $\varphi$ can be represented in the form
$$
\varphi=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_j\chi_{E_j}, \tag{4.1}
$$
where $a_j\in\mathbb R$ and $E_j\in\mathcal X$. Among these representations for $\varphi$, there is a unique standard representation characterized by the fact that the $a_j$ are distinct and the $E_j$ are disjoint nonempty subsets of $X$ and are such that $X=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}E_j$.$^{1}$
