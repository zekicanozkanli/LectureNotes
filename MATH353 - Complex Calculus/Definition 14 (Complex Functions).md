
> [!def] [[Definition 14 (Complex Functions)]]: A **complex function** on a domain $S$ is a map $f:S\to\mathbb C$, written $w=f(z)$. With $z=x+iy$ and $w=u+iv$, its real and imaginary components are $f(x+iy)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y)$, where $u(x,y)=\operatorname{Re}f(z)$ and $v(x,y)=\operatorname{Im}f(z)$.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: For $f(z)=z+1$, $f(x+iy)=(x+1)+iy$, so $u=x+1$, $v=y$; this is a translation by $1$.

> [!eg] Ex. 2: For $f(z)=iz$, $f(x+iy)=-y+ix$, so $u=-y$, $v=x$; this is the rotation pictured from the $z$-plane to the $w$-plane.

> [!eg] Ex. 3: For $f(z)=z^2$, $(x+iy)^2=(x^2-y^2)+i(2xy)$, so $u=x^2-y^2$ and $v=2xy$.

> [!eg] Ex. 4: For $f(z)=z\overline z$, $(x+iy)(x-iy)=x^2+y^2$, so $u=x^2+y^2$ and $v=0$; this function is real-valued.
