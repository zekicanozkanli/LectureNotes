
> [!def] [[Definition 15 (Complex Limits)]]: The notation $\lim_{z\to z_0}f(z)=w_0$ means that **$f(z)$ tends to $w_0$** as $z$ tends to $z_0$:
> i. For every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that $|f(z)-w_0|<\varepsilon$ whenever $0<|z-z_0|<\delta$.

> [!rem] The diagram maps a deleted $\delta$-neighborhood of $z_0$ in the $z$-plane into an $\varepsilon$-neighborhood of $w_0$ in the $w$-plane.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: $\lim_{z\to i}z^2=-1$ (see [[Exercise 3 (Limit of the Square)]]).

> [!eg] Ex. 2: $\lim_{z\to0}\overline z/z$ does not exist (see [[Exercise 4 (Conjugate Quotient Limit)]]).
