
> [!def] [[Definition 18 (Petersen Graph)]]: The Petersen graph has vertices representing the $2$-element subsets of $[5]=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$. Edges join vertices whose subsets are disjoint.

Let $A$ be an arbitrary two-element subset of $[5]$, representing a vertex of the Petersen graph. Then its neighbors are exactly the two-element subsets of $A^c.$ As $|A^c|=3,$ each vertex has degree $\binom 32 = 3.$ Thus, the Petersen graph is $3$-regular, that is, cubic. Moreover, since there cannot be three disjoint two-element subset of $[5],$ the Peterson graph is triangle-free. Furthermore, by [[Proposition 1 (Nonadjacent Vertices Shares One Neighbors in Petersen)]] it contains no $4$-cycles. And by construction, it contains two $5$-cycles. See also, [[Proposition 2 (Eight-Cycles In The Petersen Graph)]]
