
> [!def] [Definition 58 (Graphic Sequences and Realizations)](Definition%2058%20%28Graphic%20Sequences%20and%20Realizations%29.md): A **graphic sequence** is a sequence of nonnegative integers that occurs as a graph's [degree sequence](../Chapter%201/Definition%2010%20%28Degree%20Sequence%20And%20Regularity%29.md). A graph with degree sequence $S$ **realizes** $S$. Degree sequences are ordinarily written in nonincreasing order.

> [!rem] Remark. A graphic sequence of length $n$ has largest term at most $n-1$ and even sum, by [Theorem 1 (First Theorem Of The Graph Theory)](../Chapter%201/Theorem%201%20%28First%20Theorem%20Of%20The%20Graph%20Theory%29.md). These conditions alone can leave a nongraphic sequence.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: $(5,3,3,2,1)$ exceeds the largest-degree bound. $(2,2,2,2,1,1)$ is realized by $P_{6}$. $(3,3,2,2,2,2,1)$ has odd sum. For $(3,3,1,1)$, deleting a universal vertex would leave $(2,0,0)$, which has no realization.
