
> [!def] [Definition 60 (Unigraphic Sequences)](Definition%2060%20%28Unigraphic%20Sequences%29.md): A degree sequence is **unigraphic** if exactly one graph up to isomorphism realizes it.

> [!rem] Remark. A characterization of degree sequences realized by members of a class need only guarantee a realization in the class. A stronger characterization says every realization of such a sequence lies in the class.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: $(3,3,3,3)$ is realized only by $K_{4}$, whereas six copies of $3$ are realized by both $K_{3,3}$ and $K_{3}\mathbin{\square}K_{2}$.

> [!eg] Ex. 2: The sequence $(n-1,1,\ldots,1)$ is realized only by $K_{1,n-1}$. A star admits no $2$-switch.
