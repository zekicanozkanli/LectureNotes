
> [!thm] [Theorem 36 (Product Projections and Initial Topology)](Theorem%2036%20%28Product%20Projections%20and%20Initial%20Topology%29.md): For the product topology on $X\times Y$, the **projections** $p_1(x,y)=x$ and $p_2(x,y)=y$ are continuous open functions. The product topology is the smallest topology on $X\times Y$ making both projections continuous.

> [!rem] The source writes $p_1(U\times V)=U$ and $p_2(U\times V)=V$ without excluding empty factors. Those equalities require respectively $V\ne\emptyset$ and $U\ne\emptyset$; an empty rectangle has empty, hence open, images. The source asserts openness from the basic rectangles without writing the arbitrary-union step.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: Projections need not be closed: the graph of $x\mapsto\ln(x/(1-x))$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$ but projects onto $(0,1)$ (see [Exercise 93 (Projections Need Not Be Closed)](Exercises/Exercise%2093%20%28Projections%20Need%20Not%20Be%20Closed%29.md)).

###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* For $U\in\mathcal{T}$ and $V\in\mathcal{V}$, $p_1^{-1}(U)=U\times Y$ and $p_2^{-1}(V)=X\times V$ are product-open, so both projections are continuous. The source establishes openness using the images of basic rectangles. Let $\mathcal{S}=\{p_1^{-1}(U)\mid U\in\mathcal{T}\}\cup\{p_2^{-1}(V)\mid V\in\mathcal{V}\}$. Each member is a basic rectangle. Conversely, $p_1^{-1}(U)\cap p_2^{-1}(V)=(U\times Y)\cap(X\times V)=U\times V$. Thus finite intersections from $\mathcal{S}$ give the rectangular basis, so $\mathcal{S}$ is a subbase. If $\mathcal{W}$ is any topology making both projections continuous, $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{W}$, hence $\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{W}$. Therefore the product topology is initial for the two projections.$\blacksquare$
