
> [!prop] [[Proposition 8 (Density via a Basis)]]: Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ be a topological space, $\mathcal B$ a basis for $\mathcal T$, and $A\subseteq X$. Then $A$ is dense in $X$ if and only if $A\cap B\ne\emptyset$ for every {nonempty} member $B\in\mathcal B$.

> [!rem] The lecture states “for every $B\in\mathcal B$” and omits the necessary nonempty qualification, added in braces above. Its preceding finite-intersection basis explicitly contains $\emptyset$. The proof is assigned as [[Exercise 65 (Proving the Basis Density Criterion)]].

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: The irrational numbers are dense in the usual real line because they meet every nonempty open interval, as recorded in [[Definition 20 (Dense Subsets)]].

> [!eg] Ex. 2: Every nonempty subset of an indiscrete space is dense (see [[Exercise 66 (Density in an Indiscrete Space)]]).
