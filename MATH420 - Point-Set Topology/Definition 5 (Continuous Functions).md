> [!def] [[Definition 5 (Continuous Functions)]]: Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ and $(Y,\mathcal V)$ be two topological spaces, and $f:X\to Y$ be a function. We say that $f:X\to Y$, or $f:(X,\mathcal T)\to(Y,\mathcal V)$, is continuous relative to $\mathcal T$ and $\mathcal V$, or $\mathcal T$-$\mathcal V$ continuous, or simply continuous, iff the inverse image $f^{-1}(V)$ of every $\mathcal V$-open subset $V$ of $Y$ is a $\mathcal T$-open subset of $X$.
>
> That is, $f$ is $\mathcal T$-$\mathcal V$ continuous iff
> $$V\in\mathcal V\Rightarrow f^{-1}(V)\in\mathcal T.$$

^nt-5363a93814d9544a

###### Examples.

i. Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ be the discrete topological space and $(Y,\mathcal V)$ be an arbitrary topological space. Then every function $f:X\to Y$ is $\mathcal T$-$\mathcal V$ continuous.

ii. Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ be any topological space and $(Y,\mathcal V)$ be an indiscrete topological space. Then any function $f:X\to Y$ is $\mathcal T$-$\mathcal V$ continuous.

iii. In [[Exercises/Exercise 7 (Continuity Of Two Maps Between Finite Spaces)]], $f$ is continuous. But if you check, you will see that $f$ is not an open function. See [[Definition 6 (Open And Closed Functions)]].
