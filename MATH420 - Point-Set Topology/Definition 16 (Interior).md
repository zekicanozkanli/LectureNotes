
> [!def] [[Definition 16 (Interior)]]: Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ be a topological space and $A\subseteq X$. A point $x\in X$ is an **interior point** of $A$ if there exists $U\in\mathcal T$ such that $x\in U\subseteq A$. The set of all interior points is the **interior** of $A$, denoted $\operatorname{int}(A)$ or $A^\circ$.

> [!rem] Always $A^\circ\subseteq A$. A nonempty set may have empty or nonempty interior. The source diagram shows an open set around $x$ contained in $A$

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: For a singleton $A=\{x\}\subseteq\mathbb R$, the usual interior is $\emptyset$, while the discrete interior is $A$ (see [[Exercise 59 (Singleton Interiors in Two Topologies)]]).

> [!eg] Ex. 2: In the usual topology on $\mathbb R$, $[a,b]^\circ=(a,b)$, $(-\infty,a]^\circ=(-\infty,a)$, and $[a,\infty)^\circ=(a,\infty)$. In particular, a nondegenerate closed interval is not open.

> [!eg] Ex. 3: For the five-point example, $A^\circ=\{a,b\}$, $B^\circ=\{a,b,e\}$, and $C^\circ=\{a\}$ (see [[Exercise 61 (Interiors in a Five-Point Space)]]).
