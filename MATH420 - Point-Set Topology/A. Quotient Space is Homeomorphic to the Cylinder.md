
Let $X = [0, x_0] \times [0, y_0] \subset \mathbb R^2$ for $x_0, \, y_0 \gt 0$ and $\sim$ be an equivalence relation on $X$ generated by the identifications $(0, y) \sim (x_0, y)$ for all $y \in [0, y_0].$ Consider the cylinder $$C = S^1 \times [0,y_0] = \{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:u^{2}+v^{2}=1,\ 0\leq w\leq y_{0}\}.$$We equip $X$ and $C$ with the subspace topology induced from the usual topology on $\mathbb R^2$ and $\mathbb R^3,$ respectively, and equip $X /{\sim}$ with the quotient topology.

>[!thm] [[Theorem.]] $X / {\sim}$ is homeomorphic to the cylinder $S^1 \times [0, y_0].$
###### Proof
We find a homeomorphism $F^{\sim}.$
(i) Define the continuous function $F:X\to\mathbb{R}^{3}$ by $$F(x,y)=\left( \cos\frac{2\pi x}{x_0}, \sin \frac{2\pi x}{x_0}, y \right ).$$Since $\cos^{2}(2\pi x/x_{0})+\sin^{2}(2\pi x/x_{0})=1,$ we have $F(X)\subseteq C$. Conversely, for any $(u,v,w)\in C$, there exists $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$ such that $u=\cos\theta$ and $v=\sin\theta$. Hence $F\left(\frac{x_{0}\theta}{2\pi},w\right)=(u,v,w).$ Therefore, $F(X)=C$.
Moreover, for every $(x,y),(x',y')\in X$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(x,y)=F(x',y')
&\iff y=y',\ \text{and}\ \frac{2\pi x}{x_0}=\frac{2\pi x'}{x_0} + 2\pi k\ \text{for some}\ k\in \mathbb Z\\
&\iff y=y'\ \text{and}\ x-x'\in \{0, \pm x_0\}, \ \text{since}\ x, \,x' \in [0, x_0]\\
&\iff (x,y)\sim(x',y').
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the induced function
$$F^{\sim}:X/{\sim}\to C,\qquad F^{\sim}(\,[(x,y)]\,)=F(x,y)$$
is well-defined $(\Leftarrow)$, injective $(\Rightarrow)$ and surjective $(\text{since } F(X)=C).$

(ii) Let $\pi:X\to X/{\sim}$ be the natural projection. For any open subset $U\subseteq C$, there exists an open subset $V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}$ such that $U=C\cap V$. By construction, $F^{\sim}(\pi(p))=F(p)$ for every $p\in X.$ Since $F(X)=C$ and $U=C\cap V,$ we obtain
$$
\pi^{-1}\left({F^{\sim}}^{-1}(U)\right)=F^{-1}(V).
$$
Since $F$ is continuous and $V$ is open in $\mathbb R^3,$ $F^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X,$ hence ${F^{\sim}}^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X/{\sim}$. Therefore, $F^{\sim}$ is continuous.

(iii) By the Heine–Borel theorem, $X$ is compact. Since $\pi$ is continuous and surjective, $X/{\sim}=\pi(X)$ is compact. Furthermore, $C$ is Hausdorff as a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{3}$. Since every continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism, $F^{\sim}$ is a homeomorphism. Consequently,
$$
\boxed{X/{\sim}\cong S^{1}\times[0,y_{0}].}
\qquad\blacksquare
$$
