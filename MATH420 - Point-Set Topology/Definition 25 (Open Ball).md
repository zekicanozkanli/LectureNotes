
> [!def] [Definition 25 (Open Ball)](Definition%2025%20%28Open%20Ball%29.md): Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space, $x\in X$, and $\varepsilon>0$. The **open sphere**, or **open ball**, centred at $x$ of radius $\varepsilon$ is $S_d(x,\varepsilon)=\{y\in X\mid d(x,y)<\varepsilon\}=B_d(x,\varepsilon)$. When the metric is understood, write $S(x,\varepsilon)$ or $B(x,\varepsilon)$.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: For the Euclidean, maximum, and taxicab metrics on $\mathbb{R}^{2}$, balls at the origin are respectively an open disc, an open square, and an open diamond (see [Exercise 78 (Balls for Three Plane Metrics)](Exercises/Exercise%2078%20%28Balls%20for%20Three%20Plane%20Metrics%29.md)).

> [!eg] Ex. 2: For the discrete metric, $S_d(x,\varepsilon)=\{x\}$ when $0<\varepsilon\leq1$ and $S_d(x,\varepsilon)=X$ when $\varepsilon>1$ (see [Exercise 79 (Balls in a Discrete Metric)](Exercises/Exercise%2079%20%28Balls%20in%20a%20Discrete%20Metric%29.md)).
