
> [!def] [[Definition 4 (Coarser And Finer Topologies)]]: Let $\mathcal T_1$ and $\mathcal T_2$ be two topologies on a nonempty set $X$. Assume that $\mathcal T_1\subseteq\mathcal T_2$. In this case we say that $\mathcal T_1$ is **coarser**, or **smaller**, or **weaker** than $\mathcal T_2$; or that $\mathcal T_2$ is **finer**, or **larger** than $\mathcal T_1$.

> [!rem] Observe that the collection $\{\mathcal T_i\}_{i\in I}$ of all topologies on $X$ is partially ordered by class inclusion. We say that two topologies on $X$ are **not comparable** if neither is coarser than the other.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: The indiscrete and discrete topologies on $X$ are the coarsest and finest topologies on $X$, respectively.

> [!eg] Ex. 2: The usual topology is coarser than the upper-limit topology (see [[Exercise 25 (Usual and Upper-Limit Topologies)]]).

> [!eg] Ex. 3: The requested pairwise comparisons are {unresolved} (see [[Exercise 34 (Comparing Three-Point Topologies)]]).

> [!eg] Ex. 4: The two particular point topologies are incomparable and their spaces are homeomorphic (see [[Exercise 46 (Distinct Particular Point Topologies)]]).
