> [!def] [[Definition 4 (Coarser And Finer Topologies)]]: Let $\mathcal T_1$ and $\mathcal T_2$ be two topologies on a nonempty set $X$. Assume that every $\mathcal T_1$-open subset of $X$ is also a $\mathcal T_2$-open subset of $X$. In other words, $\mathcal T_1$ is a subclass of $\mathcal T_2$, i.e. $\mathcal T_1\subseteq\mathcal T_2$.
>
> In this case we say that $\mathcal T_1$ is coarser or smaller or weaker than $\mathcal T_2$, or that $\mathcal T_2$ is finer or larger than $\mathcal T_1$.
>
> Observe that the collection $\{\mathcal T_i\}_{i\in I}$ of all topologies on $X$ is partially ordered by class inclusion. We say that two topologies on $X$ are not comparable if neither is coarser than the other.

###### Examples.

i. Let $X\ne\emptyset$. What are the coarsest and finest topologies on $X$? Let $\mathcal T_1=\{\emptyset,X\}$ and $\mathcal T_2=\mathcal P(X)=\mathcal D$ be the indiscrete and discrete topologies on $X$, as in [[Definition 1 (Topological Space)]]. $\mathcal T_1$ is the coarsest topology on $X$, while $\mathcal T_2$ is the finest topology on $X$. This tells us that if $\mathcal T$ is an arbitrary topology on $X$, then $\mathcal T_1\subseteq\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T_2$.
