
> [!def] [Definition 31 (Equivalence Relations and Classes)](Definition%2031%20%28Equivalence%20Relations%20and%20Classes%29.md): An **equivalence relation** $\sim$ on $X$ is a relation satisfying:  
> i. **Reflexivity:** $x\sim x$ for every $x\in X$.  
> ii. **Symmetry:** $x\sim y$ implies $y\sim x$ for all $x,y\in X$.  
> iii. **Transitivity:** $x\sim y$ and $y\sim z$ imply $x\sim z$ for all $x,y,z\in X$.  
> For such a relation, the **equivalence class** of $x\in X$ is $[x]=\{y\in X\mid x\sim y\}$.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: Congruence modulo $3$ has classes $[0]=\{0,\pm3,\pm6,\pm9,\ldots\}$, $[1]=\{1,4,7,10,\ldots,-2,-5,\ldots\}$, and $[2]=\{2,5,8,11,\ldots,-1,-4,-7,\ldots\}$. Each integer belongs to exactly one of them, and every equivalence class equals one of these three classes.

> [!eg] Ex. 2: Pairing opposite vertical edges of a rectangle with reversed height gives the classes described in [Exercise 94 (Rectangle Partition with Reversed Heights)](Exercises/Exercise%2094%20%28Rectangle%20Partition%20with%20Reversed%20Heights%29.md).

> [!eg] Ex. 3: Pairing the same edges at equal height gives the classes described in [Exercise 95 (Rectangle Partition with Equal Heights)](Exercises/Exercise%2095%20%28Rectangle%20Partition%20with%20Equal%20Heights%29.md).
