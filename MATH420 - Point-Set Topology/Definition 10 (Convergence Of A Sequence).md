
> [!def] [[Definition 10 (Convergence Of A Sequence)]]: Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ be a topological space and $(x_n)$ be a sequence of points in $(X,\mathcal T)$. We say that $(x_n)$ converges to a point $x_0\in X$ if for every $U\in\mathcal T$ containing $x_0$, there exists $n_0\in\mathbb N$ such that for every $n \ge n_0 \ x_n\in U$. That is to say, if $U$ contains almost all terms (all but finitely many terms) of the sequence, we say $(x_n)$ converges to $x_0$. We write $\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n=x_0$.

It is important to note that the collection of convergent sequences is insufficient to describe topological spaces. To see this, recall that the co-countable and discrete topologies on an uncountable set $X$ are different, however, the collection of convergent sequences in these spaces are the same, see [[Exercise 13 (Convergence In A Discrete Space)]]) and [[Exercise 14 (Convergence In A Cocountable Space)]]). For another perspective, see [[Theorem 3 (Continuous Functions Preserve Convergence)]].
