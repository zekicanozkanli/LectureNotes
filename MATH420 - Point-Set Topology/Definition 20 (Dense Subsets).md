
> [!def] [[Definition 20 (Dense Subsets)]]: Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ be a topological space and $A\subseteq X$. The set $A$ is **dense** in $X$ if $\overline A=X$.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: Every nonempty subset of an indiscrete space is dense (see [[Exercise 66 (Density in an Indiscrete Space)]]).

> [!eg] Ex. 2: In a discrete space, a subset is dense if and only if it equals $X$ (see [[Exercise 67 (Density in a Discrete Space)]]).

> [!eg] Ex. 3: In the usual topology on $\mathbb R$, the irrational numbers $\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q$ form a dense subset. The class $\mathcal B=\{(a,b)\mid a,b\in\mathbb R,\ a<b\}$ is a basis and every member contains at least one irrational number. Hence $(\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q)\cap(a,b)\ne\emptyset$ for every $(a,b)\in\mathcal B$, and [[Proposition 8 (Density via a Basis)]] gives $\overline{\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q}=\mathbb R$.

> [!eg] Ex. 4: The relation $U\subseteq\overline{A\cap U}$ for dense $A$ and open $U$ has no supplied proof: {unresolved} (see [[Exercise 50 (Dense Sets Within Open Sets)]]).

> [!eg] Ex. 5: The density of $\mathbb Z$ in the particular point topology on $\mathbb Q$ with distinguished point $1$ is {unresolved} in the supplied material (see [[Exercise 55 (Integers in Particular Point Topology)]]).
