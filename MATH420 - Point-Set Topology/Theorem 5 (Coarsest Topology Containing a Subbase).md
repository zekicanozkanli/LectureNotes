
> [!thm] [[Theorem 5 (Coarsest Topology Containing a Subbase)]]: Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ and $(X,\mathcal T\prime)$ be topological spaces, and let $\mathcal S$ be a subbase for $\mathcal T$. If $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal T\prime$, then $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T\prime$. 
> 

Thus the topology generated by a subbase is the coarsest topology containing that subbase.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: A common subbase determines equal topologies (see [[Exercise 26 (Uniqueness from a Common Subbase)]]).

###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* Assume $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal T\prime$. Since $\mathcal T\prime$ is closed under finite intersections, $\bigcap\mathcal S\prime\in\mathcal T\prime$ for every finite subclass $\mathcal S\prime\subseteq\mathcal S$, so $\mathcal S^{\cap}\subseteq\mathcal T\prime$. Since $\mathcal T\prime$ is also closed under arbitrary unions, $\bigcup\mathcal B\prime\in\mathcal T\prime$ for every $\mathcal B\prime\subseteq\mathcal S^{\cap}$. But $$\mathcal T=\left\{\bigcup\mathcal B\prime\mid\mathcal B\prime\subseteq\mathcal S^{\cap}\right\},$$ hence $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T\prime$.$\blacksquare$
