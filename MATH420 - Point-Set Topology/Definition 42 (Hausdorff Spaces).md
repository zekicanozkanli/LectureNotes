
> [!def] [Definition 42 (Hausdorff Spaces)](Definition%2042%20%28Hausdorff%20Spaces%29.md): A topological space $(X,\mathcal{T})$ is a **$T_2$-space**, or **Hausdorff space**, if:  
> i. Every pair of distinct points $x,y\in X$ has disjoint open sets $U,V\in\mathcal{T}$ with $x\in U$ and $y\in V$.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: Every metric space is Hausdorff (see [Theorem 22 (Metric Spaces Are Hausdorff)](Theorem%2022%20%28Metric%20Spaces%20Are%20Hausdorff%29.md)).

> [!eg] Ex. 2: The cofinite topology on an infinite set is $T_1$ and is not Hausdorff (see [Exercise 102 (Cofinite Spaces Are Not Hausdorff)](Exercises/Exercise%20102%20%28Cofinite%20Spaces%20Are%20Not%20Hausdorff%29.md)).
