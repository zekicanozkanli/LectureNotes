
> [!def] [Definition 41 (T1 Spaces)](Definition%2041%20%28T1%20Spaces%29.md): A topological space $(X,\mathcal{T})$ is a **$T_1$-space** if:  
> i. For every pair of distinct points $x,y\in X$, there exist $U,V\in\mathcal{T}$ such that $x\in U$, $y\notin U$, $x\notin V$, and $y\in V$.

> [!rem] Each point lies in an open set that does not contain the other point. The sets $U$ and $V$ need not be disjoint.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: The topology $\{\emptyset,\{a\},\{a,b\},X\}$ on $X=\{a,b,c\}$ is $T_0$ but not $T_1$ (see [Exercise 100 (Separation in Three-Point Spaces)](Exercises/Exercise%20100%20%28Separation%20in%20Three-Point%20Spaces%29.md)).

> [!eg] Ex. 2: A topology is $T_1$ exactly when it contains the cofinite topology (see [Exercise 101 (T1 via the Cofinite Topology)](Exercises/Exercise%20101%20%28T1%20via%20the%20Cofinite%20Topology%29.md)).

> [!eg] Ex. 3: On an infinite set, the cofinite topology is $T_1$ but not Hausdorff (see [Exercise 102 (Cofinite Spaces Are Not Hausdorff)](Exercises/Exercise%20102%20%28Cofinite%20Spaces%20Are%20Not%20Hausdorff%29.md)).
