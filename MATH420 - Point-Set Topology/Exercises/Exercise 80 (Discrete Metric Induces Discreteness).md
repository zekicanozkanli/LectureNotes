
> [!ex] [Exercise 80 (Discrete Metric Induces Discreteness)](Exercise%2080%20%28Discrete%20Metric%20Induces%20Discreteness%29.md): Show that the topology induced by a discrete metric is the discrete topology.

###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* By [Exercise 79 (Balls in a Discrete Metric)](Exercise%2079%20%28Balls%20in%20a%20Discrete%20Metric%29.md), $B_d(x,\varepsilon)=\{x\}$ for $0<\varepsilon\leq1$ and $B_d(x,\varepsilon)=X$ for $\varepsilon>1$. Hence the metric neighbourhood system of $x$ is $\{N\subseteq X\mid \{x\}\subseteq N\text{ or }X\subseteq N\}=\{N\subseteq X\mid x\in N\}$. This is the neighbourhood system of $x$ for the discrete topology by [Exercise 74 (Neighbourhoods in Discrete Spaces)](Exercise%2074%20%28Neighbourhoods%20in%20Discrete%20Spaces%29.md). Uniqueness in [Theorem 17 (Topology from Neighbourhood Systems)](../Theorem%2017%20%28Topology%20from%20Neighbourhood%20Systems%29.md) gives equality of the topologies.$\blacksquare$
