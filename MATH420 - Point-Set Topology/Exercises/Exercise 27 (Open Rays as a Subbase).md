
> [!ex] [[Exercise 27 (Open Rays as a Subbase)]]: Let $(\mathbb R,\mathcal T)$ have the usual topology and let $$\mathcal S=\{(-\infty,b)\mid b\in\mathbb R\}\cup\{(a,\infty)\mid a\in\mathbb R\}.$$ Show that $\mathcal S$ is a subbase for $\mathcal T$.

###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* Let $\mathcal T\prime$ be the topology on $\mathbb R$ generated by $\mathcal S$. Recall that $\mathcal B=\{(a,b)\mid a,b\in\mathbb R,\ a<b\}$ is a basis for $\mathcal T$. We show $\mathcal T=\mathcal T\prime$. For $a<b$, $$(a,b)=(-\infty,b)\cap(a,\infty)\in\mathcal T\prime,$$ because $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal T\prime$ and $\mathcal T\prime$ is closed under finite intersections. Arbitrary unions of these basis elements also belong to $\mathcal T\prime$, so $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T\prime$. Conversely, for any $a,b\in\mathbb R$, $$(-\infty,b)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}(b-n-2,b-n),\qquad(a,\infty)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}(a+n,a+n+2).$$ Thus both kinds of rays are unions of members of $\mathcal B$, giving $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal T$. By [[Theorem 5 (Coarsest Topology Containing a Subbase)]], $\mathcal T\prime\subseteq\mathcal T$. Hence equality holds and $\mathcal S$ is a subbase for $\mathcal T$.$\blacksquare$
