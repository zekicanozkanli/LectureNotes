# Exercise 19 (The Usual Topology Is Strictly Coarser Than The Discrete Topology)

Let $X=\mathbb R$, and let $\mathcal D$ and $\mathcal T$ be the discrete topology and the usual topology on $\mathbb R$, respectively. Show that $\mathcal T\subsetneq\mathcal D$.

^nt-1a47de6c481ae198

###### Proof.

> [!proof]
> First note that $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal D$. Since $\mathcal D$ is the discrete topology on $\mathbb R$, $\{x\}\in\mathcal D$ for every $x\in\mathbb R$, by [[Definition 1 (Topological Space)]]. But $\{x\}$ is not $\mathcal T$-open. Otherwise, we would be able to express $\{x\}$ as a union of open intervals in $\mathbb R$, which is impossible, by [[Definition 13 (Usual Topology On The Real Line)]] and [[Definition 12 (Basis For A Topology)]].
>
> Therefore $\mathcal T\subsetneq\mathcal D$.
