
> [!def] [Definition 40 (T0 Spaces)](Definition%2040%20%28T0%20Spaces%29.md): A topological space $(X,\mathcal{T})$ is a **$T_0$-space** if:  
> i. For every pair of distinct points $x,y\in X$, there exists $U\in\mathcal{T}$ such that either $x\in U$, $y\notin U$, or $x\notin U$, $y\in U$.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: On $\{a,b,c\}$, $\{\emptyset,\{a\},\{b,c\},X\}$ is not $T_0$, whereas $\{\emptyset,\{a\},\{a,b\},X\}$ is $T_0$ (see [Exercise 100 (Separation in Three-Point Spaces)](Exercises/Exercise%20100%20%28Separation%20in%20Three-Point%20Spaces%29.md)).
