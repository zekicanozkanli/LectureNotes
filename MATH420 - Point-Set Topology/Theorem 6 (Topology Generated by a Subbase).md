
> [!thm] [[Theorem 6 (Topology Generated by a Subbase)]]: Any class $\mathcal S$ of subsets of a nonempty set $X$ is a subbase for a unique topology $\mathcal T$ on $X$.

Thus the theorem gives a construction for the smallest topology containing the chosen sets.
Also note that by *unique*, we mean that $\mathcal S$ generates one and only one topology $\mathcal T$ and not that $\mathcal T$ cannot be generated by a collection $\mathcal S'$ other than $\mathcal S.$ The generating map is not one-to-one in general.

###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* Let $\mathcal B=\mathcal S^{\cap}$. To prove that $\mathcal B$ is a basis for a topology on $X$, verify the two conditions in [[Theorem 4 (Conditions For A Collection To Form A Basis)]]. The empty class $\mathcal A=\{A_i\mid i\in\emptyset\}$ is a finite subclass of $\mathcal S$, and $\bigcap\mathcal A=X$. Hence $X\in\mathcal B$, so $\bigcup\mathcal B=X$. Let $B_1,B_2\in\mathcal B$ and $x\in B_1\cap B_2$. There are finite subclasses $\mathcal S_1,\mathcal S_2\subseteq\mathcal S$ with $B_1=\bigcap\mathcal S_1$ and $B_2=\bigcap\mathcal S_2$. Put $\mathcal S_3=\mathcal S_1\cup\mathcal S_2$. This is a finite subclass of $\mathcal S$, so $B_3=\bigcap\mathcal S_3\in\mathcal B$ and $x\in B_3\subseteq B_1\cap B_2$. Explicitly, if $B_1=S_1\cap\cdots\cap S_n$ and $B_2=S_1\prime\cap\cdots\cap S_m\prime$, then $$\mathcal S_3=\{S_1,\ldots,S_n,S_1\prime,\ldots,S_m\prime\},\qquad B_3=S_1\cap\cdots\cap S_n\cap S_1\prime\cap\cdots\cap S_m\prime.$$ The topology exists, and uniqueness follows from [[Exercise 26 (Uniqueness from a Common Subbase)]].$\blacksquare$
