
> [!def] [[Definition 15 (Subbase)]]: Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ be a topological space. A subclass $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal T$ is a **subbase** for $\mathcal T$ if the finite intersections of members of $\mathcal S$ form a basis for $\mathcal T$. 
> 

To be super clear, define $$\mathcal S^{\cap}=\left\{\bigcap\mathcal S'\mid\mathcal S'\subseteq\mathcal S,\ \mathcal S'\text{ finite}\right\},$$ 
that is, take every possible finite subclass $\mathcal S'$ of $\mathcal S$, take the intersection of all members of each $\mathcal S'$, and collect them to form $\mathcal S^{\cap}$, with the convention* $\bigcap \emptyset = X$ (by letting the index set $I=\emptyset$). Then $\mathcal S$ is a subbase for $\mathcal T$ if and only if $\mathcal S^{\cap}$ is a basis for $\mathcal T$.
Equivalently, for every $U\in\mathcal T$ and every $x\in U$, there are finitely many $S_1,\ldots,S_n\in\mathcal S$ such that $x\in S_1\cap\cdots\cap S_n\subseteq U$.

($*$): For an indexed family $\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ of subsets of $X$, we have $\bigcap_{i \in \emptyset}A_i = X$. 
###### Motivation.
A class $\mathcal S$ of subsets of a nonempty set $X$ may fail to be a basis, but it always generates a topology by first taking finite intersections and then arbitrary unions.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: Let $X=\{a,b,c\}$, $A_1=\{a,b\}$, and $A_2=\{b,c\}$. The collection $\mathcal S=\{A_1,A_2\}=\{\{a,b\},\{b,c\}\}$ is a subbase for the topology
> $$\mathcal T=\{\emptyset,\{b\},\{a,b\},\{b,c\},\{a,b,c\}\}.$$
> One possible finite subclass is $\mathcal S'=\{A_1\}=\{\{a,b\}\}$, whose intersection is $\bigcap\mathcal S'=\{a,b\}$. Taking all possible finite intersections, including the empty intersection $X$, gives
> $$\mathcal S^{\cap}=\{X,A_1,A_2,A_1\cap A_2\}=\{\{a,b,c\},\{a,b\},\{b,c\},\{b\}\}.$$
> Taking arbitrary unions of members of $\mathcal S^{\cap}$ gives precisely $\mathcal T$; thus $\mathcal T$ is the topology generated by $\mathcal S$.

> [!eg] Ex. 2: Let $X=\mathbb R$, $A_1=(-\infty,2)$, and $A_2=(0,\infty)$. The collection $\mathcal S=\{A_1,A_2\}$ is a subbase for the topology
> $$\mathcal T=\{\emptyset,(0,2),(-\infty,2),(0,\infty),\mathbb R\}.$$
> Choose the finite subclass $\mathcal S'=\{A_1,A_2\}=\mathcal S$. Then $\bigcap\mathcal S'=(0,2)$. Taking all possible finite intersections gives
> $$\mathcal S^{\cap}=\{\mathbb R,(-\infty,2),(0,\infty),(0,2)\}.$$
> Taking arbitrary unions of these sets gives precisely $\mathcal T$, the topology generated by $\mathcal S$. The subclass $\mathcal S'$ has two members, although both members are infinite sets.

> [!eg] Ex. 3: Let $X=\mathbb R$ and define $A_n=(-1/n,1/n)$ for each $n\in\mathbb N_{>0}$, where $\mathbb N_{>0}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$. The infinite collection $\mathcal S=\{A_n:n\in\mathbb N_{>0}\}$ is a subbase for the topology
> $$\mathcal T=\{\emptyset,\mathbb R\}\cup\left\{\left(-\frac1n,\frac1n\right):n\in\mathbb N_{>0}\right\}.$$
> Choose the finite subclass $\mathcal S'=\{A_2,A_5\}$. Then
> $$\bigcap\mathcal S'=A_2\cap A_5=A_5=\left(-\frac15,\frac15\right).$$
> Because the intervals are nested, every nonempty finite intersection equals the interval with the largest index among those selected. Including the empty intersection gives
> $$\mathcal S^{\cap}=\{\mathbb R\}\cup\{A_n:n\in\mathbb N_{>0}\}.$$
> Every nonempty union of the $A_n$'s equals the selected interval with the smallest index. Thus arbitrary unions of members of $\mathcal S^{\cap}$ give precisely $\mathcal T$, the topology generated by $\mathcal S$. Although $\bigcap\mathcal S=\{0\}$, this is an infinite intersection, and $\{0\}$ belongs to neither $\mathcal S^{\cap}$ nor $\mathcal T$.

> [!eg] Ex. 4: The left and right open rays form a subbase for the usual topology (see [[Exercise 27 (Open Rays as a Subbase)]]).

> [!eg] Ex. 5: Every basis is a subbase (see [[Exercise 45 (Every Basis Is a Subbase)]]).
