
> [!prop] [Proposition 09 (Integral Top Homology and Orientability)](Proposition%2009%20%28Integral%20Top%20Homology%20and%20Orientability%29.md): For a triangulated closed connected surface $K$, $H_2(K;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$ if the surface is orientable and $H_2(K;\mathbb{Z})=0$ otherwise.

###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* In an integral $2$-cycle the coefficients on the two triangles incident to an edge must cancel with the signs induced at the edge. Thus the coefficients agree up to sign. Propagating through the connected surface, a nonzero cycle has constant nonzero absolute coefficient and determines consistent orientations by its signs. Conversely, when consistent orientations exist, the sum of the consistently oriented triangles is a cycle and generates $Z_2$. There are no $3$-simplices, so $H_2=Z_2$. $\blacksquare$
