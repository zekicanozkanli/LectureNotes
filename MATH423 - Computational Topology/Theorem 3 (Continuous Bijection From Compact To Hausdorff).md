
> [!thm] [[Theorem 3 (Continuous Bijection From Compact To Hausdorff)]]: Every continuous bijection from a compact space $X$ to a Hausdorff space $Y$ is a homeomorphism.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: Let $I=[0,1]$ with $0\sim1$. The continuous map $f(t)=(\cos(2\pi t),\sin(2\pi t))$ identifies exactly the endpoints and induces a continuous bijection $\overline{f}:I/{\sim}\to S^1$. The quotient is compact by [Theorem 2 (Continuous Image Of A Compact Space)](Theorem%202%20%28Continuous%20Image%20Of%20A%20Compact%20Space%29.md) applied to the projection of $I$, and $S^1$ is Hausdorff. Therefore $\overline{f}$ is a homeomorphism. This contrasts with the noncompact domain $[0,2\pi)$ in [Definition 21 (Homeomorphism)](Definition%2021%20%28Homeomorphism%29.md).
