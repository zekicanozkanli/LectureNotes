
> [!def] [Definition 17 (Hausdorff Space)](Definition%2017%20%28Hausdorff%20Space%29.md): A topological space $X$ is **Hausdorff** if for every pair of distinct points $x,y\in X$ there are open sets $U\ni x$ and $V\ni y$ with $U\cap V=\emptyset$.

> [!rem] Limits of sequences are unique in Hausdorff spaces. Not every topology is induced by a metric: the trivial topology on a set with at least two points has no disjoint open neighborhoods separating distinct points.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: Every metric space is Hausdorff: for $x\ne y$, the open balls of radius $r=d(x,y)/2$ centered at $x$ and $y$ are disjoint. In particular, every subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is Hausdorff.

> [!eg] Ex. 2: The **line with two origins** is formed from two copies of $\mathbb{R}$ by identifying corresponding nonzero points. It is locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$, but its origins cannot be separated by disjoint open sets. It is not a manifold.
