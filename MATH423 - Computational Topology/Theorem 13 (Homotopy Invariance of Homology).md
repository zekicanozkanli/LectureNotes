
> [!thm] [Theorem 13 (Homotopy Invariance of Homology)](Theorem%2013%20%28Homotopy%20Invariance%20of%20Homology%29.md): If the polyhedra $\lvert K\rvert$ and $\lvert L\rvert$ are homotopy equivalent, their simplicial homology groups are isomorphic in all degrees, both over $\mathbb{Z}$ and over $\mathbb{Z}/2$. In particular a homeomorphism has this consequence.

> [!rem] The lecture states this without proof and points to simplicial approximation in Munkres’s Elements of Algebraic Topology. For Midterm 1, invariance of Betti numbers under change of triangulation may be used without proof.

###### Examples.
> [!eg] Ex. 1: The boundaries of the tetrahedron and octahedron both triangulate the sphere and have $b=(1,0,1)$. A cone is contractible and has the homology of a point. The [Theorem 09 (Nerve Theorem)](Theorem%2009%20%28Nerve%20Theorem%29.md) theorem and homotopy invariance give the homology of the union of the radius-$\epsilon$ balls from its Čech complex.
