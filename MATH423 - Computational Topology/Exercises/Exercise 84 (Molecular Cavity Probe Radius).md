
> [!ex] [Exercise 84 (Molecular Cavity Probe Radius)](Exercise%2084%20%28Molecular%20Cavity%20Probe%20Radius%29.md): Worksheet D6. For the toy protein of eleven atoms of radius $1.7\,\text{Å}$ on a circle of radius $6\,\text{Å}$ with one site missing, explain why the pocket closes at inflated radius $R=3\,\text{Å}$ and fills at $R=6\,\text{Å}$. Give the probe-radius range with a cavity.

###### Proof.
> [!pf] *Proof.* Consecutive atoms are $12\sin 15^\circ\approx3.11\,\text{Å}$ apart, so their original disks already meet. The atoms flanking the missing site, at $30^\circ$ and $330^\circ$, are $6\,\text{Å}$ apart, so their disks meet at $R=3\,\text{Å}$ and close the mouth. The center is at distance $6\,\text{Å}$ from every atom and is the last point of the cavity covered. Thus $3\leq R<6$ and, with $R=1.7+\rho$, $1.3\leq\rho<4.3$ in ångströms. $\blacksquare$
